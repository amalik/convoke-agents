# Evaluator interview guide — 15 minutes, before the framing locks

**Goal:** learn what decision these evaluating organisations are actually making, before we write page one of the whitepaper.
**Deadline:** both interviews done by **Thursday 2026-09-17**. After that the framing is locked and the answers can't change the document.
**Who:** two of the evaluating organisations, ideally one enthusiastic and one skeptical.

## Before the call
- Don't send the product, a deck or the docs. We want their frame, not a reaction to ours.
- Tell them: *"15 minutes. I'm writing a document for people in your position and I want it to answer your questions, not mine."*

## The call (15 min)

**1. The decision (4 min)**
- "When you say you're evaluating BMAD and Convoke, what's the decision on the table? Who makes it?"
- "What happens if you decide *yes*? And if you decide *no*?"
- *Listen for:* pilot vs. practice-wide rollout, who signs, what it replaces.

**2. The last time (4 min)**
- "Tell me about the last time your organisation adopted a new delivery method or AI tool. What made it work, or stall?"
- "What did the people who had to approve it ask for?"
- *Listen for:* past behaviour, not hypotheticals. The evidence they asked for then is the evidence they'll ask for now.

**3. The stakes (4 min)**
- "Of adoption, scaling, governance, portability and interoperability, which one would stop this if it went wrong?"
- "What would you need to see to be confident about it?"
- *Listen for:* the ONE stake that matters most to them. That's our page-one candidate.

**4. The no (2 min)**
- "What would make you walk away?"

**5. Close (1 min)**
- "If a document answered one question for you, which one?"

## Rules for you
- Don't pitch, don't correct, don't defend. If they're wrong about the product, write it down: that misunderstanding is data.
- Ask "tell me more" and "why" before moving on.
- Silence is fine. Let them fill it.
